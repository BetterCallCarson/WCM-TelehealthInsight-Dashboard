Predicting past-year telehealth use

Verified snapshot · NHIS 2024–2025

In 2025, the primary model ranked a telehealth user above a nonuser about 72% of the time. Its unweighted AUROC of 0.7173 exceeded the full Andersen model by 0.0134. Group lasso (minimum loss) and XGBoost scored only 0.0044 and 0.0041 higher than the primary model.

AUROC by model and year

From one survey year to the next

2024 · Develop and test

31,877 adults met the completeness criteria. An outcome-stratified split assigned 25,500 to training and 6,377 to testing. Stepwise BIC selected 23 predictor terms for the primary logistic model.

2025 · Evaluate the fixed model

A new NHIS sample provided 23,605 eligible adults. Each respondent reported their own past-year use. The team applied the 2024 model without refitting.

Discrimination and probability error

AUROC measures how often a user ranks above a nonuser, with ties receiving half credit. Lower Brier scores indicate smaller squared probability errors.

Calibration in 2025

The weighted share reporting use was 26.26%. The model predicted 25.62% on average. The gap was +0.65 percentage points (design-based 95% CI −0.15 to +1.44).

Scope and limitations

The 2025 evaluation measures performance in a new sample from the same survey. Earlier iterations examined both evaluation samples. Screening and the outcome-proxy diagnostic preceded cross-validation, and XGBoost tuning was outside the nested comparison.

Complete-case requirements differ from the population trends. The survey weights do not correct for exclusions specific to this model.
